01

Project Overview

This project emerged from an assignment asking us to explore the real-world consequences of trade policy through storytelling and structured argumentation. Two works were created: a speculative fiction narrative set in Bangladesh 2043, and a structured debate guide on autarky. Together, they demonstrate why nations that close themselves off from global trade tend to suffer — even when the intention is self-reliance.

📖

What Inspired This

An assignment combining narrative writing with economic theory — using speculative fiction to model what happens when a nation abandons international trade and imposes economic isolation.

⚖️

What Concept Is Demonstrated

Autarky — a policy of economic self-sufficiency that eliminates or minimizes foreign trade. The project explores why autarky fails across multiple dimensions: supply chains, innovation, crisis response, and welfare.

🌐

Why It Matters

Trade is not optional infrastructure — it's a survival network. Comparative advantage, supply chain resilience, and technology transfer all depend on openness. Autarky severs these lifelines.

🔑

Core Argument

Even under autarky's own goals — maximum resilience, survival continuity — interdependence often outperforms isolation because it expands the solution space for novel crises.

"Autarky purchases predictable degradation at the cost of unpredictable recovery."

An autarkic nation trades the risk of external dependency for the risk of internal capability ceilings. In a world of complex crises — pandemics, floods, technology gaps — internal ceilings are often the more dangerous constraint. This project traces exactly how that plays out through the fictional lens of Bangladesh in 2043.

This is an excerpt from the structured debate assignment. The AI (DAH-Snarky) plays devil's advocate for autarky; the student (You) argues for interdependence. Click exchanges to expand them.

01 DAH-Snarky You're redefining "ideal" into something no system can meet…
↯ DAH-Snarky — Pro-Autarky
If "ideal" means maximal capability in all conditions with zero trade-offs, then sure — autarky isn't ideal. But neither is global trade. Neither is anything physical. You've set a criterion that collapses every strategy equally. Autarky's real case: we retain usable capability when external systems degrade. Trade systems buy higher average capability and faster innovation — but introduce external dependency as a single point of failure. Calling autarky "not ideal" only works if you assume ideal = maximizes capability everywhere.
02 You Autarky reduces external risk, but increases internal limitation risk…
⬡ You — Pro-Interdependence
I'm not defining ideal as "perfect in all cases." I'm defining it as the system that gives the highest overall chance of survival across different types of crises. Autarky reduces external risk, but it increases internal limitation risk. Because it trades one type of failure for another, it cannot be the ideal system — it is just one side of a trade-off.
03 DAH-Snarky "It's a trade-off, therefore not ideal" is a non sequitur…
↯ DAH-Snarky — Pro-Autarky
Saying "it's a trade-off, therefore it cannot be ideal" is a non sequitur. Everything becomes disqualified under that logic. The existence of trade-offs doesn't decide optimality; it defines the search space. In survival theory, continuity is not a side variable. It's often the variable that decides whether the system is still participating in the game at all. External-risk systems fail in correlated ways. Internal-limited systems fail in degraded ways — but degrade gracefully.
04 You Continuity without sufficient capability doesn't guarantee survival either…
⬡ You — Pro-Interdependence
Yes, autarky gives continuity — but if the system lacks enough capability in some crises, that continuity doesn't guarantee survival. The 2047 Bangladesh flood is the clearest example: foreign aid arrived at the border, the capability existed in the network — but autarky's own rules prevented accessing it. Continuity of an isolated state is not survival. It's just a slower path to collapse.
05 DAH-Snarky A three-way impossibility constraint — you can only shift weight between them…
↯ DAH-Snarky — Pro-Autarky
You cannot maximize peak capability AND eliminate external dependency AND guarantee survival across all extreme scenarios simultaneously. A system with higher theoretical capability that cannot access it under stress doesn't outperform a lower-capability system that can actually deploy. At best, you've shown that no single axis dominates all extreme cases — which pushes toward "survival optimization is about balancing failure modes," not eliminating one side and declaring the problem solved.

Story Evidence — The Closed Delta
Bangladesh 2043 · Speculative Narrative Assignment
Act II — The Trade Wars
The USA taxed Chinese goods. China replied. India taxed Bangladeshi clothes, and Bangladesh responded. Soon many countries were fighting with trade, and global trade became weak. Silong Sala's rule stopped working. — Direct illustration of how retaliatory tariffs create welfare losses across all participants.
Act III — The Demagogue
"We don't need other countries." — The government closed borders, stopped imports, and limited foreign money. Bangladesh became isolated. This mirrors historical autarky cases: North Korea post-1950s, Myanmar 1960–1988, Maoist China 1949–1978.
Act IV — The Collapse
Medicine disappeared. Machines broke. Power plants stopped. Farmers had rice, but tractors failed. — Demonstrates intermediate goods dependency: even food production requires imported inputs. No modern economy is truly autarkic in its supply chain.
Act V — The Flood (2047)
A big flood destroyed everything. Other countries sent help, but it stopped at the border — because foreign aid was not allowed. — The most powerful illustration: autarky's rules blocked survival resources that already existed at the border.
Epilogue — Recovery
Trade returned slowly. The economy improved. "Closing the country was a big mistake." — The economist's verdict validates the core thesis: openness is not vulnerability. It is the foundation of resilience.

What Did I Learn?

📚
Knowledge
  • Comparative advantage explains why trade generates surplus for all parties even when one party is more productive at everything
  • Autarky's definition distinguishes trade from dependence — and that distinction is precisely where its internal logic breaks down in practice
  • Intermediate goods mean that even "basic" production (food, medicine) is globally interdependent at the supply chain level
  • Historical cases — North Korea, Maoist China, Myanmar — show that autarky consistently produces welfare loss and technology stagnation
⚙️
Skills
  • Structured argumentation — building and attacking positions using defined criteria
  • Scenario modeling — translating economic theory into narrative and simulation
  • Critical thinking — identifying when an argument's premises undermine its own conclusions
  • Data visualization — converting qualitative outcomes into comparative visual evidence
  • Trade-off analysis — thinking in systems rather than in single-dimension optimization
💡
Reflection

The hardest part was the debate format. DAH-Snarky's point that "trading one failure mode for another doesn't disqualify either system" forced me to think more carefully about evaluation criteria. I initially argued that trade was simply better — but I had to learn to argue on autarky's own terms first.

What surprised me most was how the narrative format made the economic consequences more visceral than a table of statistics ever could. The flood scene — aid stopped at the border — crystallized the concept instantly.

"Closing the country was a big mistake."
— Silong Sala, Economist · The Closed Delta, Bangladesh 2043

🌍 Real-World Application

China's partial decoupling from Western tech supply chains (2019–present) shows that even the world's second-largest economy cannot fully insulate itself from interdependence. Semiconductor self-sufficiency remains elusive despite massive state investment.

🏭 Supply Chain Lesson

COVID-19 (2020–2022) demonstrated both sides simultaneously: countries with diverse trade networks had more sourcing options for PPE and vaccines; countries with high concentration on single suppliers were most vulnerable.

🔬 Pandemic Attack Vector

The pandemic vaccine scenario from the debate — where critical synthesis ingredients are distributed across five countries — is not hypothetical. mRNA vaccine production genuinely requires globally distributed rare chemical inputs that no single nation produces independently.

🌾 Bangladesh Context

Bangladesh's real economy is one of the great success stories of export-led growth. The RMG (ready-made garment) sector, built on comparative advantage, lifted millions out of poverty. The scenario in the story is a direct inversion of this actual history.

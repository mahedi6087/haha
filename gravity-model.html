01 / Project Overview

What this project explores

// Inspiration

The assignment that drove this

An empirical assignment asked students to select their own country pairs, source real bilateral trade data, apply the gravity equation, and identify where the model succeeds — and where it fails catastrophically.

// Concept

Gravity model of international trade

Trade between two countries is predicted to grow proportionally with the product of their GDP sizes and shrink inversely with geographic distance — directly mirroring Newton's gravitational law applied to economics.

// Importance

Why failures are the real finding

The model's largest residuals are its most informative outputs. A 98% prediction gap between India and Pakistan reveals political and historical forces that no economic variable — not GDP, not distance — can capture.

// The gravity equation — baseline form
Tij = A × (Yi × Yj) / Dij
TijPredicted bilateral trade volume — USD billions per year
YiGDP of country i in USD billions
YjGDP of country j in USD billions
DijDistance between capitals in kilometres
ACalibration constant — absorbs political, cultural and historical factors

🇮🇳 🇳🇵 India – Nepal

The formula nearly perfectly predicted this pair. Nepal is economically integrated with India — open borders, currency corridors, deep supply-chain dependence, and zero political disruption. This represents the model's ideal conditions: pure economic gravity, no friction.

🇮🇩 🇵🇰 Indonesia – Pakistan

Despite 5,220 km of ocean, the fit is strong. Both share Islamic trade diplomacy norms and no formal barriers. This shows that distance alone does not destroy accuracy — what matters is the absence of political friction embedded in A.

✗ Largest anomaly
Residual: −$76.20 bn

🇮🇳 🇵🇰 India – Pakistan

Model predicts $77.4bn; actual trade is $1.24bn. Three wars, the Kashmir conflict, formal import bans, and severed transport links collapse trade to 1.6% of the predicted figure. Derived A = 0.00051 — the dataset's lowest by a factor of 17.

✗ Second largest anomaly
Residual: −$34.37 bn

🇮🇳 🇧🇩 India – Bangladesh

Geographic proximity suggests strong trade, but non-tariff barriers, large informal flows that evade official records, Bangladesh's Western export orientation, and immigration-related political tensions suppress the bilateral figure to 28% of prediction.

04 / Reflection

What I learned

Knowledge

Core concepts acquired

  • The gravity model is one of the most empirically reliable equations in economics — elegant in form, powerful in practice, yet revealing in its failures.
  • The calibration constant A is not a flaw — it is the model's most interesting variable, encoding diplomacy, culture, history, and policy into a single number.
  • Residuals are not errors. They are windows into forces that economics alone cannot explain — particularly political friction and historical conflict.
  • Even highly open borders reduce trade substantially. National boundaries impose frictions far beyond tariffs: information asymmetry, legal complexity, and trust deficits persist.
The model's power lies not in what it can explain — but in what it cannot.
Skills

Capabilities developed

  • Data sourcing — collecting bilateral trade figures from OEC and World Bank across 12 self-selected country pairs.
  • Quantitative modelling — applying a multi-variable formula to real data, back-calculating constants, and evaluating model fit systematically via residuals.
  • Outlier reasoning — distinguishing random model error from structured anomalies driven by identifiable geopolitical forces.
  • Statistical judgment — recognising that a simple mean of A was distorted by political outliers, requiring trimming or weighting for robust estimation.
Reflection

Surprises & real-world links

Most surprising: India and Pakistan hold the exact economic and geographic profile for $77bn in annual trade. Geopolitics alone reduces this by 98%. No economic variable assigned to either country changes that figure.

Hardest methodological challenge: Choosing a defensible A when derived constants ranged 200× across pairs. A simple average is misleading when dominated by political outliers.

Real-world link: Governments and central banks use gravity models to project how proposed FTAs will shift bilateral flows before signing. India–UAE CEPA (2022) and ASEAN expansion negotiations both used gravity-based projections as baseline evidence for negotiations.

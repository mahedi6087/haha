The Gravity Model of Trade

Two economies trade in proportion to their size and in inverse proportion to the distance between them — exactly as gravity pulls masses. This equation explains why neighbouring giants dominate each other's import lists, and why efficiently allocating trade flows requires understanding the physics-like forces that shape them.

Ricardex — Comparative Advantage

Even a country worse at producing everything gains from trade by focusing on what it gives up least to make. Ricardo's insight — that relative cost, not absolute ability, drives specialization — is the foundational evidence that free exchange maximizes mutual benefit even between unequal partners.

Resource Abundance & Trade Patterns

The Heckscher-Ohlin theorem: countries export goods that use their abundant factor intensively. Efficient resource allocation at global scale means labour-rich nations specialize in labour-intensive goods and capital-rich ones in capital-intensive goods — letting each economy do what its endowment makes cheapest.

Systems Thinking & Economies of Scale

When unit costs fall as output rises, trade is no longer just about differences — it's about size and feedback. Systems thinking reveals how increasing returns and network effects concentrate production, creating self-reinforcing loops that reshape the geography of industry and make optimized scale a source of sustained competitive value.

Self-sufficiency carries a hidden price: a closed economy must produce everything domestically, surrendering the welfare gains that efficient international specialization delivers. This project maps the deadweight loss of autarky and asks when, if ever, the security premium justifies the allocative inefficiency of isolation.

Binary Decision Optimizer

When resource allocation requires a hard yes-or-no — build or don't, hire or don't — binary integer programming finds the combination that maximizes value within budget and capacity constraints. This project demonstrates why rounding a continuous LP solution is dangerous, and why proper 0-1 programming is essential evidence-based management.

LP Solver

Linear programming finds the allocation of resources that maximizes profit or minimizes cost subject to constraints. The simplex algorithm navigates the corners of the feasible polytope until it reaches the global optimum — turning complex multi-variable trade-offs into rigorous, evidence-guided decisions rather than guesswork.

Branch and Bound

When an LP optimum turns out fractional but the problem demands whole-number decisions, branch and bound constructs a decision tree: branch on a fractional variable, solve LP sub-problems, and prune branches whose bound can't beat the best integer solution found. Systematic, guaranteed optimal, and a model of evidence-guided elimination.

Travelling Salesman Problem

Visit every city exactly once and return home at minimum cost. With 20 cities there are more possible tours than atoms in the observable universe — a combinatorial explosion that makes brute force impossible. This project applies exact methods and heuristic strategies to make an NP-hard allocation problem tractable and practically useful.

Workforce Planning

Matching staff levels to fluctuating demand is a multi-period resource allocation problem: too few workers and service quality collapses; too many and fixed costs destroy margins. This project formulates workforce planning as a linear program with shift, overtime, and coverage constraints — converting an intuitive scheduling problem into an optimized, evidence-based model.

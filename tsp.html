01 · Project Overview

What is the TSP?

A delivery driver must visit every town and return to the warehouse — what's the shortest possible route? Simple to state, surprisingly complex to solve.

🏭

The Scenario

A warehouse (WH) dispatches deliveries to towns A, B, C, D. Fuel costs money, so the shortest round-trip matters.

📈

Why It Matters

TSP underpins real-world logistics: courier routing, circuit board drilling, DNA sequencing, and supply chain optimization.

🧮

The Challenge

With n cities, there are (n-1)!/2 possible routes. As n grows, brute-force becomes impossible — smarter algorithms are needed.

🔗

The Concept

TSP teaches graph traversal, Hamiltonian circuits, combinatorial explosion, and why optimization heuristics exist.

04 · What Did I Learn?

Reflections

Three dimensions of learning from working through the Traveling Salesman Problem.

Knowledge

Graph Theory & Combinatorics

TSP is a Hamiltonian circuit problem on a weighted complete graph. With 5 nodes, there are 4! = 24 orderings to check. Symmetric TSP means A→B equals B→A, halving unique routes. Finding the provably optimal solution is NP-hard — no algorithm guarantees both speed and optimality as cities grow.

Skills

Optimization & Decision Trees

Working through brute-force manually built intuition for why heuristics exist. I practiced systematic enumeration using a decision tree, learned to prune branches early when a partial path already exceeds the known best, and applied nearest-neighbor heuristics before verifying with brute force.

Reflection

Surprises & Real-World Ties

It surprised me that four different routes all share the same optimal cost of 25. The C→D block exercise showed how fragile an optimal solution can be — one broken road raises cost by 20%. Real-world logistics needs backup routes for exactly this reason.
